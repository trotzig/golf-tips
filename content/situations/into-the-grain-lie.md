title: Into-the-grain lie
summary: Grass growing toward you, against the shot — it grabs the club, so the ball comes out slower and shorter.

level: all
clubs: [wedges, irons, hybrids]
setup:
  - Read the grass — dull, dark blades leaning toward you mean you're into the grain.
  - Play the ball a little back of center.
  - Grip a little firmer so the grass can't twist the face.
  - Choose a lofted club with some bounce.
swing:
  - Swing a little steeper to reach the ball before the grass.
  - Swing much harder than the distance suggests — a chip from the rough may need nearly twice the swing.
  - Accelerate through; the grass will slow the club down.
expect:
  - The ball comes out slower, carries shorter and stops sooner.
  - Grass can grab the hosel and close the face, pulling it {left}.
  - Full shots from the fairway lose a little distance.
mistakes:
  - Making your normal chip and leaving it in the rough or fringe.
  - Decelerating, which lets the grass stop the club.
  - Trying to putt or sweep the ball through it.
club_choice: More loft and a bigger swing around the green; from the fairway, one club more.
drill: Chip from the same spot into and with the grain, and compare how far each one goes.
advanced: Grain is strongest on Bermuda and other warm-season grass. It often grows toward the setting sun or toward drainage.

Grain is the direction the grass blades lie. Into the grain, the blades point back at you, so the
club has to push through them on the way to the ball. Around the green this is where most short
chips come from: a stroke that would run to the hole from a normal lie can barely get out of the
rough. Commit to a firmer swing than feels natural.
