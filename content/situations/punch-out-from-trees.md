---
title: Punching out from trees
summary: Blocked by trees — take your medicine with a low punch back to the fairway instead of the hero shot.
category: rough-and-trouble
tags: [recovery, trajectory, course-management, safety]
aliases: [stuck behind a tree, chip out sideways, ball in the woods]
level: all
clubs: [irons, hybrids]
setup:
  - Find the widest, lowest gap back to the fairway, even if it's sideways.
  - Check overhead branches for your backswing and follow-through.
  - Take a less-lofted iron, such as a 6- or 7-iron, to keep the ball low.
  - Play the ball back in your stance with your hands ahead.
swing:
  - Make a short, controlled backswing — about waist to chest high.
  - Keep your hands ahead through impact and hold a low, short finish.
expect:
  - A low shot that runs a long way once it lands.
  - A full next shot from the fairway, usually costing just one stroke.
mistakes:
  - Trying the narrow gap toward the green, which often costs two or three shots.
  - Hitting so hard that the ball runs through the fairway into more trouble.
club_choice: Pick the club that keeps the ball under the branches, then swing it gently to control the run.
advanced: Aim to leave a full-swing distance with your favorite club for the next shot.
related: [low-shot, thick-rough, laying-up, after-a-bad-hole]
viz: punch-out-from-trees
updated: 2026-10-31
---

Safety first: balls can rebound hard off trunks and branches. Choose a gap well away from the
nearest trunk and make sure no one is standing in the trees nearby.
